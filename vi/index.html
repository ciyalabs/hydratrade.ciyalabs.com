HydraTrade VI: Where the Leverage Went

Funding on the gold and equity perpetuals of a crypto exchange, two trades that do not travel, and the carry that does

Loghman SamaniCiya LabsNovember 2026

Funding on a perpetual is the rent a leveraged buyer pays to a patient seller. Over the last three months, buyers on a large crypto exchange paid about 7 percent a year to hold bitcoin this way, and about 19 percent to hold gold.

This report follows that rent across the exchange's newer contracts on gold, silver, oil, stock indices and single stocks, in three rounds that were each written down before they ran. The first two looked for a gap between two contracts on the same ounce of gold. Both earned money on the exchange where they were found and lost it on a second exchange, so the gap belongs to one venue's crowd and was not kept. The third round held tokenised gold and shorted the gold perpetual. It earned 15.9 percent a year after costs over 74 days, 11.9 percent over ten months on the second exchange, and 11.7 points a year more than bitcoin and ether carry over the same days.

Ithe rent

Leverage has a price, and the crowd sets it

A perpetual future never expires, so the exchange ties its price to the underlying asset with a periodic payment between longs and shorts. When the perpetual trades above the asset, longs pay. The size of that payment is about as direct a market price for leverage as finance offers.

He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter read funding this way, as the cost of a leveraged long that arbitrageurs are paid to absorb.1 The idea is older than crypto. Frazzini and Pedersen show that investors who cannot borrow freely bid up whatever gives them leverage, from high beta stocks to options and leveraged funds, and accept lower returns for it.2,3 Whoever sells that leverage earns the difference. Attention decides which leverage is wanted. Barber and Odean find that individual investors buy what has caught their eye,4 and a gold price making new records is about as eye-catching as markets get.

Why carry, not predictionThe trades here make no forecast of the price of gold. Each holds the same ounce long and short, so it earns the funding and the small drift between two prices, and nothing else.

Report five measured this rent on bitcoin and ether. It paid 34 percent a year in 2021 and about 2 percent so far in 2026. The ribbon above suggests where the demand went. Through 2026 the exchange listed perpetuals on gold, silver, oil, stock indices and single stocks, and from the summer on nearly all of them charged longs far more than bitcoin did. Carry earns what the scarce side is paid, as Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt show across asset classes,5 and in crypto that has been leverage.6 So the question was a practical one. Can the cash and carry that works on bitcoin and ether be run on gold, and does it beat them on the same capital?

IIround one

Four contracts, one ounce

The exchange lists four perpetuals that track gold. One follows the metal's price directly. Two follow tokens that are each backed by an ounce held in a vault, XAUT and PAXG. The fourth follows a token of a gold fund. Over the same months they charged very different rents.

Gârleanu and Pedersen showed that the same asset can trade at different prices when its claims need different margin or attract different buyers, and that the gap lasts as long as arbitrage capital is short.7 Shleifer and Vishny had already explained why it often is.8 Lamont and Thaler found such gaps even between a parent company and its own listed subsidiary.9 Round one tested the plainest version. Every day it shorted whichever gold contract had charged the most over the past week and bought whichever had charged the least, as long as the gap was above 5 percent a year and each contract traded at least $50k a day.

It earned 17.1 percent a year after costs from October 2025, with a t statistic of 2.75 and gains in both halves of the year. Most of that came from one place. The gold fund token's contract traded tens of thousands of dollars a day, and its funding swung from minus 116 to plus 149 percent a year between months. Whenever it cleared the floor it was the short. Without a floor the rule earned 44 percent, and at a floor of $200k it earned 0.6 percent. The liquid gold contract, the one charging a steady 20 percent, was held on only 16 days.

The rule also failed its replication. A second exchange lists the same kind of contracts on gold and the two tokens. On those the rule lost 4.1 percent a year, and its trailing gap told nothing about the next week's (an R² of 0.00, against 0.27 on the first exchange).

The other groupsThe Nasdaq pair earned 52 percent over 54 days with no floor, and 10 percent at $50k. Two crudes earned 72 percent, all of it from the spread between them. A long and short book across equity contracts lost 33 percent a year, because their prices moved far more than their funding paid.

A wide gap on a thin contract is a price that nobody has been paid enough to correct.

That is the finding of report five in a new market. The funding spread between exchanges was widest where almost nobody traded, and here the gap between gold contracts was widest on the contract that almost nobody traded.

IIIround two

A gap that belongs to one venue

Round two kept only the two gold contracts that trade at size, the metal at about $1.9M a day and XAUT at about $0.9M in the last week of the sample. It shorted the first and bought the second in equal amounts, always, whatever the funding said.

On the first exchange this earned 10.6 percent a year over 73 days, with a t of 2.22, gains in both halves and positive funding in 82 percent of weeks. The two legs never drifted apart by more than 0.3 percent in a day. As a trade it was close to ideal, quiet and cheap to hold.

It did not travel. The second exchange has listed the same two contracts since March 2026, and there they charged about the same, so the pair lost 1.5 percent a year over 205 days. The gap was a property of one exchange's buyers, who reach for the contract named after gold and pay more for it than for the token beside it. Makarov and Schoar found crypto prices segmented by the clienteles of different venues,10 and a gap that lives in one venue's clientele can close whenever that venue's arbitrageurs notice it. The pair was registered as failing.

IVround three

Renting out the ounce

The pairs asked whether two gold contracts disagree. The archive pointed to a simpler question. Do gold perpetuals charge their buyers a premium over gold itself, on more than one exchange?

Commodity futures normally price the cost of carrying the physical asset, mostly interest and storage,11 and gold is cheap to store. A perpetual that charges 20 percent a year while financing costs a few percent is charging for something else, and the natural reading is demand for leverage. On the second exchange the gold perpetual has charged 12.7 percent a year since December 2025, while bitcoin and ether charged about 2.7 percent there. Both readings point the same way.

Round three held XAUT and shorted the same number of ounces in the gold perpetual. It was measured against the bitcoin and ether carry over the same days, because the two compete for the same capital and gold has to beat it, not merely earn something. From 13 August it earned 15.9 percent a year after a full round trip of costs, with a t of 3.12, 17.0 and 14.8 percent in the two halves, positive funding in every week and a deepest drawdown of 0.3 percent. The bitcoin and ether carry earned 4.2 percent on the same days, so the difference was 11.7 points a year, with a t of 2.53. On the second exchange, shorting its gold perpetual against gold earned 11.9 percent a year over 311 days. All five conditions of the pass rule held.

A longer windowThe only gold carry with a full year behind it uses the PAXG token's own perpetual, which the gold crowd uses less. It earned 6.2 percent against 2.8 percent for bitcoin and ether, and lost money in 3 of its 13 calendar months.

Retail access then decides what can be done. At the retail tier the exchange's spot market sells PAXG but not XAUT, and PAXG counts as futures collateral at 93 percent of its value. Retail spot fees are 0.40 percent for resting orders and 0.80 percent for crossing ones, higher than the earlier studies assumed. At those fees the PAXG version earned 13.2 percent a year with a t of 1.94, and the bitcoin and ether carry 1.7 percent. Both pay the same spot fees, so the gap between them did not change.

Three rounds on one family of contracts invite the worry Harvey, Liu and Zhu raise about searching until something works.12 Two things temper it here. Every round was registered before it ran, and the result that passed is the one whose economics replicate on a second exchange, while the two that failed are the two that do not.

Vone capital

Replace, or stack

A cash and carry earns because it owns the asset. The capital that owns bitcoin cannot also own PAXG, so two spot carries on one capital always split it. Only the pair, which needs margin and no spot, can sit on top.

So a capital that already runs bitcoin and ether carry has two ways to take gold on. It can replace that carry with gold carry, or keep it and add the gold pair from round two on margin. Figure 4 follows the three choices over the 73 days on which every leg traded.

Neither choice strains the margin. At the exchange's retail rates a short in the bitcoin or ether perpetual needs about 1 percent of its notional as initial margin and the gold short about 4, against coins that count as collateral at 99 percent and PAXG at 93. Adding the pair at twice the capital lifts the initial margin to about 13 percent of the capital.

The two rest on different evidence. The pair's extra return comes from a gap the second exchange does not show, and at twice the capital most of it sits on one young contract. Gold carry earns a premium that both exchanges charge, and the token perpetuals, which the gold crowd uses less, have charged about 7 percent a year while bitcoin charged 3.5. Retail leverage limits also differ by asset. European rules for contracts for difference cap retail leverage at 2 to 1 on crypto and 20 to 1 on gold,13 so if perpetuals were treated as such contracts the crypto legs would bind long before the gold ones.

VIwhat now

What this cannot show

Gold carry met every condition it was given, on 74 days of one exchange and ten months of another. That is enough to take it seriously and too little to call it settled.

The limits are plain. The primary window is 74 days, and the longer evidence comes from a second exchange and a thinner contract, at lower rates. Gold carry is a regime trade in the same way bitcoin carry was. It pays while leveraged buyers crowd into gold, and the 2025 to 2026 rally is what brought them. Neither window can show risks that never appeared in it, such as a contract being delisted or a token losing its link to the metal. And switching between carries is not free, since a round trip through the spot market costs about 1.25 percent.

A test that cannot be tuned is already set. The round three rule is fixed, so every day of data after 26 October 2026 is out of sample for it. Around the end of January 2027 the same rule will be run on three new months. If the gold crowd thins and the bitcoin crowd comes back, the two carries can trade places, though a switch only pays when the new gap outlasts the 1.25 percent a round trip costs.

Mmethods

How the numbers were produced

Data

Hourly funding, hourly candles, hourly mark prices and the spot index of every gold, commodity, index and equity perpetual on the first exchange, from each contract's listing to 26 October 2026, and the same for its bitcoin and ether contracts. Daily spot prices of XAUT and PAXG on its spot market. Funding, mark and index prices of the gold, PAXG, XAUT, bitcoin and ether perpetuals on the second exchange, from March 2025. The last decision day is 25 October 2026.

The rules

Decisions are taken daily at midnight UTC from funding printed before it. In round one the signal is each eligible contract's mean funding over one, seven or thirty days, annualised. A contract is eligible when it has funding over the whole window and a median daily dollar volume over the previous week above a floor of $0, $50k or $200k. The arm shorts the richest and buys the cheapest when the gap exceeds a threshold of 0, 5 or 10 percent a year and holds while it stays above half of that. The equity group holds three a side. Round two holds a fixed pair always, or only while its trailing gap is positive. Round three holds the spot token against the gold perpetual always. P&L is the funding each leg receives or pays at its own hourly prints, plus the long leg's price return less the short leg's.

Costs

Perpetual fees of 0.02 percent for resting orders and 0.05 for crossing ones, blended 60 to 40, with a half spread of 3 basis points and 1 of slippage per side. Spot fees of 0.25 and 0.40 percent in the registered runs, and 0.40 and 0.80 percent, the retail tier's, in the sensitivity. The second exchange charges 0.10 percent on spot. Rules held always pay one entry and one exit within their span.

Statistics

Every round was registered before it ran, with its pass rule, and is reported whether it passed or failed. t statistics use a Newey and West variance on daily returns.14 The best of the 108 variants of round one is deflated for selection.15 Every figure is drawn by a script from the stored results.
